In the last episode, we looked at counting heads in a coin toss—a discrete experiment. But what happens when we measure things like height, temperature, or the time between two bus arrivals?

These are **Continuous Random Variables**.

## The Probability Density Function (PDF)

For continuous RVs, we can't assign probability to a single point (it's always zero!). Instead, we assign probability to **intervals**. This is described by the PDF, $f(x)$.

> [!TIP]
> The area under the entire PDF curve must always equal 1.

### The Normal Distribution

Perhaps the most famous distribution of all is the Normal (or Gaussian) distribution. It is symmetric, bell-shaped, and defined by its mean ($\mu$) and standard deviation ($\sigma$).

## Why is it so "Normal"?

The **Central Limit Theorem (CLT)** tells us that if we sum up enough independent random variables, the result will be Normal, regardless of the original distribution!
