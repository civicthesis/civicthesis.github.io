In the high-stakes poker game of Bihar politics, **Samrat Choudhary** brought a helmet. Or rather, a **Muratha** (turban). He vowed never to take it off until he removed Nitish Kumar from the Chief Minister's chair.

The universe, having a sick sense of humor, made him the Deputy Chief Minister *under* Nitish Kumar.

## The Quantum Turban

Schrödinger’s cat is both dead and alive. Samrat’s turban is both a symbol of rebellion and submission.

Every morning, he stands before the mirror, adjusting the saffron cloth.
**Inner Voice:** "You swore to remove him!"
**Outer Voice:** "Yes, sir, I’ll sign the file immediately, sir."

Scientists are studying the fabric of his turban. They believe it contains **Dark Matter**, which is the only thing dense enough to absorb such massive political U-turns without imploding.

## The "Doctor" Strange

Samrat Choudhary is also a man of medicine (allegedly). His degrees are as mysterious as the Bermuda Triangle or the actual number of bridges in Bihar that haven't collapsed yet.

He prescribes "bulldozers" for headaches and "party switching" for career fatigue. When asked about the side effects of his policies, he simply tightens his turban. "The pressure helps me think," he says.

## The Nitish Paradox

The biggest challenge Samrat faces is the **Nitish Paradox**: *Nitish Kumar moves towards you faster than you can move him out.*

Every time Samrat prepares a coup, Nitish walks into the BJP office with a bouquet and a smile, saying, "I have returned (again)."
Samrat clenches his fist, realizing he has to wait another term to unwind his head.

## The Final Unwinding

Legend says that if Samrat ever takes off his turban, the gravitational balance of Bihar will shift.
- The bridges might stop falling.
- The liquor ban might actually work.
- Tejashwi might finish his 10th grade.

But until then, the Turbanator remains, a warrior fighting a battle against a boss he cannot fire, armed with nothing but a cloth and a very confused ambition.
