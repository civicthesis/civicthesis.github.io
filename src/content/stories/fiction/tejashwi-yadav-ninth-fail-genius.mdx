In the annals of history, there are geniuses who dropped out of college: Bill Gates, Mark Zuckerberg, Steve Jobs. And then there is **Tejashwi Yadav**, who looked at the 10th-grade board exam and said, "Nah, I’m good. I have a legacy to inherit."

While the youth of Bihar were busy preparing for UPSC in 10x10 rooms without ventilation, Tejashwi was busy preparing for the IPL (Indian Political League), where the toss is fixed and the umpire is your uncle.

## The Waterboy Paradox

Tejashwi’s cricket career is the stuff of legend. He was part of the Delhi Daredevils squad for four years. He played zero matches. He scored zero runs. Yet, he earned crores.

Economists call this **"Passive Income."** 
Bihar calls it **"Forecasting the Future."**

He carried drinks with such grace that the ICC considered introducing a "Best Hydration Manager" award. Realizing that carrying bottles was too much manual labor for a future CM, he pivoted to a field where the only thing you have to carry is your father’s baggage.

## The "Jobs for Land" Exchange Program

Tejashwi revolutionized HR practices with his innovative barter system. Why rely on boring entrance exams when you can use the **Real Estate Barter System**?

It was a visionary move to solve land scarcity and unemployment simultaneously. Critics called it a "Scam." Tejashwi called it "Asset-Backed Employment Generation."

## Rebranding "Jungle Raj"

When accused of bringing back "Jungle Raj," Tejashwi didn't deny it. He hired a PR firm from South Delhi to rebrand it.

"It’s not Jungle Raj," he explained in a press conference, wearing a Gucci muffler. "It is an **Eco-Friendly Organic Governance Model.** We are returning to nature. Why do you need paved roads when you have SUVs? Why do you need streetlights when you have inner enlightenment (and stolen electricity)?"

## The 10-Lakh Jobs Promise

During the election, he promised 10 lakh jobs with a pen stroke. When asked how, he smiled the smile of a man who knows mathematics is just a social construct.

"I will sign the file," he said.
"But where is the budget?" asked a journalist.
"Budget?" Tejashwi laughed. "My father managed the railways with a profit by doing magic tricks with accounting. You think I can't manage salaries? We will pay in 'exposure' and 'blessings'."

## The Legacy

Today, Tejashwi stands as a beacon of hope for every backbencher. He proved that you don't need to pass 9th grade to decide the curriculum of PhD students. You don't need to know physics to understand the gravity of power.

He is the **Schrödinger’s Politician**: He is both in power and in opposition, depending on which way Nitish Kumar wakes up in the morning.

As Bihar moves forward (or sideways, or in circles), Tejashwi remains the eternal Youth Icon—aging, but never graduating.
