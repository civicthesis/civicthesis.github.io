It wasn't the landslide victory that shocked everyone; it was the *silence*. For the first time in Delhi’s history, the celebratory firecrackers didn’t go off. Not because of the ban—nobody listens to those—but because the fog was so thick, the guys with the matches couldn’t find the fuses.

In that silent, grey haze, **Rekha Gupta** walked into the Secretariat, not as a politician, but as a woman who had simply had *enough*.

## The "Saas-Bahu" Doctrine

Her first executive order wasn't about the metro or the Yamuna foam. It was personal. She declared that all Resident Welfare Associations (RWAs) would now be legally classified as "Combat Zones."

The city cheered. Parking disputes dropped by 90% as residents realized their aggressive neighbors were actually surprisingly frail when not protected by a gate.

## The Smog Solution

Scientists had proposed artificial rain, giant vacuums, and odd-even schemes. Rekha Gupta proposed something darker: **The "Survival of the Fittest" Air Tax.**

The logic was simple: "If you want to breathe premium air, move to Switzerland. If you're in Delhi, you earn your oxygen."

She replaced all air purifiers in government offices with potted plants that looked vaguely judgmental. "If the plant dies," she told her cabinet, "so does your career." Productivity skyrocketed as terrified bureaucrats spent their days misting ferns instead of pushing files. The AQI didn't improve, but the *vibes* were impeccable.

## The Yamuna froth "Cappuccino" Initiative

When asked about the toxic foam on the Yamuna during Chhath Puja, CM Gupta didn't deflect. She rebranded.

"It's not pollution," she announced at a press conference, wearing a hazmat sari. "It's an artisanal, chemically-infused foam experience. Delhi is the only city offering a river bath and a chemical peel simultaneously."

Tourism spiked. Influencers flocked to the riverbanks for the "Toxic Glow" aesthetic. The hospitals filled up shortly after, but strictly speaking, the economy *was* moving.

## The Final Act

Rekha Gupta’s tenure ended as abruptly as it began. On her 100th day, she tried to solve the ultimate Delhi problem: **Traffic at the Ashram Flyover.**

She didn't widen the road. She didn't add a lane. She simply removed the traffic lights and replaced them with a giant screen playing *Bigg Boss* highlights.

Drivers were so mesmerized by the on-screen fighting that they forgot to honk. Traffic halted completely. The silence returned.

In that stillness, Rekha Gupta resigned. "My work here is done," she reportedly said, putting on an N95 mask over her regular mask. "I'm moving to Gurgaon. I hear the power cuts there give you time to think."

 Delhi remains, as always, a gas chamber of dreams. But for a brief, coughing moment, it had a leader who looked the apocalypse in the eye and said, *"Thoda adjust kar lo."*
