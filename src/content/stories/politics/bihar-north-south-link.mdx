Prashant Kishor’s **"River Grid System"** is essentially a statewide plumbing project designed to solve two opposite problems at once: the devastating annual floods in the North and the parched, cracked earth of the South.

By linking the perennial Himalayan rivers with the seasonal rivers of South Bihar, his plan targets two specific geographical clusters.

### 1. The "Surplus" Zone: North Bihar

In the current system, districts in North Bihar act as a massive drainage basin for water coming from the Nepal hills. These districts would benefit by having their "excess" water diverted into the grid rather than overtopping embankments.

<Aside type="warn" title="Key Districts">
  **The Seemanchal Region:** Supaul, Saharsa, Madhepura, Araria, Purnia, Katihar, and Kishanganj.
</Aside>

Currently, these areas face massive **crop loss and land erosion**. The grid would stabilize the water levels of the Kosi and Gandak, turning these <ScribbleHighlight color="blue">"Sorrowful Rivers"</ScribbleHighlight> into a steady supply for the rest of the state.

### 2. The "Deficit" Zone: South Bihar

South Bihar relies mostly on rainfall and seasonal rivers that go bone-dry by March. PK’s grid would bring a "pipeline" of North Bihar’s surplus water to these districts.

*   **Key Districts:** Gaya, Aurangabad, Nawada, Jehanabad, Arwal, and parts of Jamui and Banka.
*   **The Benefit:** These districts often see **mass migration** because farming is impossible for half the year. A permanent water supply would allow for <ScribbleHighlight color="green">three-crop cycles</ScribbleHighlight> (Kharif, Rabi, and Zaid) instead of just one.

### The "Connecting" Corridors

The blueprint focuses on creating specific "Water Highways." One of the most discussed links in this context is the **Kosi-Mechi link**, but PK’s vision extends further:

1.  **North-to-South Channels:** Linking the Kosi or Gandak directly to the Falgu or Punpun rivers via a series of underground or elevated canals.
2.  **Storage Hubs:** Building massive reservoirs in districts like **Rohtas and Kaimur** where the topography allows for natural water storage, acting as a "buffer" during dry months.
