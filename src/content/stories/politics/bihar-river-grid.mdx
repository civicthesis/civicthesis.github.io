Prashant Kishor (PK), through his political platform <ScribbleHighlight color="yellow">Jan Suraaj</ScribbleHighlight>, has proposed a comprehensive **"River Grid System"** to address the recurring cycle of floods and droughts in Bihar. His approach shifts the focus from *controlling* rivers to *managing* water as a resource.

### 1. The Core Philosophy: From Liability to Asset

Currently, Bihar views its rivers (especially those coming from Nepal) as a "curse" or liability during the monsoon. Kishor argues that water should be treated as a valuable economic asset.

> The "River Grid" is designed to ensure that excess water doesn't just destroy land but is stored and moved to where it is needed, effectively **monetizing** Bihar's largest resource.

### 2. Integrated "One Grid" Framework

Instead of separate projects for different rivers (like the Kosi Plan or Gandak Plan), PK proposes a <ScribbleHighlight color="blue">Unified Statewide Water Management Strategy</ScribbleHighlight>.

*   **River Linking:** The grid would involve linking the major rivers of North Bihar (which are prone to flooding) with the rivers of South Bihar (which often face droughts).
*   **Horizontal and Vertical Integration:** Water would be moved not just from North to South, but also across East to West through a network of canals and reservoirs, creating a "grid" similar to an electricity grid.

### 3. Solving the Triple Crisis

PK points out that Bihar suffers from three distinct but related water problems. The grid system aims to balance these by taking the surplus from flood zones and transferring it to deficit drought zones.

### 4. Moving Away from "Embankment Culture"

Kishor is a vocal critic of the current system of building and repairing embankments (*bandh*).

*   **The Corruption Argument:** He claims that the <ScribbleHighlight color="pink">"embankment-contractor nexus"</ScribbleHighlight> thrives on floods. Embankments are often deliberately weakened or poorly maintained so that they breach, allowing for massive "flood relief" and "repair" funds to be siphoned off.
*   **The Engineering Flaw:** He argues that embankments have actually worsened floods by preventing natural silt deposition and causing riverbeds to rise higher than the surrounding land.

### 5. Inspiration from the "China Model"

PK often cites China’s success in managing its massive river systems. China developed a sophisticated river grid that turned historically "sorrowful" rivers into engines of agricultural growth. He suggests that with modern engineering and a "master plan" that spans the entire state (rather than piecemeal repairs), Bihar can achieve similar results.

### Summary of Benefits

| Feature | Traditional Approach | Prashant Kishor’s Grid Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Strategy** | Build embankments (Control) | Link rivers & create reservoirs (Management) |

| **Goal** | Stop water from entering land | Redirect and store water as an asset |
