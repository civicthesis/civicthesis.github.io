## The Concept of a Limit

Calculus is the study of change. At its core lies the **Limit**.

<CinematicText 
  text="Approaching... but never touching." 
  font="serif" 
  animation="blur-reveal" 
  className="text-xl text-center italic my-6 text-gray-500"
/>

<HandDrawnBox title="Definition" variant="note">
  We say $\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = L$ if we can make $f(x)$ arbitrarily close to $L$ by taking $x$ sufficiently close to $c$.
</HandDrawnBox>

### Visualizing Limits

Imagine walking towards a door. You take steps that are half the distance to the door.
1. 1/2 way there.
2. 3/4 way there.
3. 7/8 way there.

You get infinitely close, but theoretically, you never hit the door. That's a limit.

## Continuity

A function is continuous if you can draw its graph without lifting your pen.

<CinematicCard theme="neon" title="Conditions for Continuity">
  <p>A function $f(x)$ is continuous at $x=c$ if:</p>
  <ol class="list-decimal list-inside mt-4 space-y-2">
    <li>$f(c)$ is defined.</li>
    <li>$\lim_{x \to c} f(x)$ exists.</li>
    <li>$\lim_{x \to c} f(x) = f(c)$.</li>
  </ol>
</CinematicCard>

## The Derivative

The derivative measures the **instantaneous rate of change**.

<CinematicText 
  text="SLOPE OF THE TANGENT" 
  font="bold-sans" 
  animation="shake" 
  color="#f59e0b"
  className="text-2xl text-center my-8"
/>

$$f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}$$

<div class="grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-2 gap-6 my-8">
    <CinematicCard theme="paper" title="Velocity">
        If $s(t)$ is position, then $s'(t)$ is velocity.
    </CinematicCard>
    <CinematicCard theme="paper" title="Acceleration">
        If $v(t)$ is velocity, then $v'(t)$ is acceleration.
    </CinematicCard>
</div>
