## What is a Proposition?

In mathematics, we deal with statements that are either **True** or **False**. These are called propositions.

<CinematicCard theme="paper" title="Definition">
  <p>A <strong>proposition</strong> is a declarative statement that is either true or false, but not both.</p>
  <div class="scribble-highlight mt-4 inline-block">Key Concept</div>
</CinematicCard>

### Examples

<div class="grid grid-cols-1 md:grid-cols-2 gap-4 my-8">
    <CinematicCard theme="noir" title="Valid Propositions">
        <ul>
            <li>The sun rises in the East. (True)</li>
            <li>2 + 2 = 5. (False)</li>
            <li>New Delhi is the capital of India. (True)</li>
        </ul>
    </CinematicCard>
    <CinematicCard theme="neon" title="Not Propositions">
        <ul>
            <li>What is your name? (Question)</li>
            <li>Close the door. (Command)</li>
            <li>x + 2 = 5. (Depends on x)</li>
        </ul>
    </CinematicCard>
</div>

## Logical Connectives

We can combine propositions using logical connectives.

<CinematicText 
  text="AND, OR, NOT" 
  font="bold-sans" 
  animation="glitch" 
  color="#ef4444"
  className="text-3xl text-center my-8"
/>

<HandDrawnBox title="Truth Tables" variant="tip">
  Let $p$ and $q$ be two propositions.
  
  | $p$ | $q$ | $p \land q$ (AND) | $p \lor q$ (OR) | $\neg p$ (NOT) |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | T | T | T | T | F |
  | T | F | F | T | F |
  | F | T | F | T | T |
  | F | F | F | F | T |
</HandDrawnBox>

## Implication ($p \implies q$)

This is one of the most important connectives. It is read as "if $p$, then $q$".

<CinematicText 
  text="IF P THEN Q" 
  font="mono" 
  animation="tracking-in" 
  shadow="soft"
  className="text-2xl text-center my-8"
/>

<HandDrawnBox title="The Promise Analogy" variant="danger">
  Think of $p \implies q$ as a promise.

  - If I promise ($p$) to give you candy, and I give you candy ($q$), I kept my promise (True).
  - If I promise ($p$), but don't give you candy (not $q$), I broke my promise (False).
  - If I didn't promise (not $p$), whether I give you candy or not, I didn't break any promise (True).
</HandDrawnBox>
