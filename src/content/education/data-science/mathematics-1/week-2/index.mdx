## Relations

A relation defines a connection between elements of two sets.

<CinematicText 
  text="CONNECTING DOTS" 
  font="bold-sans" 
  animation="tracking-in" 
  color="#8b5cf6"
  className="text-2xl text-center my-8"
/>

<HandDrawnBox title="Cartesian Product" variant="note">
  The Cartesian product of sets A and B, denoted $A \times B$, is the set of all ordered pairs $(a, b)$ where $a \in A$ and $b \in B$.
  
  $$A \times B = \{(a, b) \mid a \in A, b \in B\}$$
</HandDrawnBox>

### Types of Relations

1.  **Reflexive:** Every element is related to itself. $(a, a) \in R$ for all $a \in A$.
2.  **Symmetric:** If $a$ is related to $b$, then $b$ is related to $a$.
3.  **Transitive:** If $a$ is related to $b$ and $b$ is related to $c$, then $a$ is related to $c$.

<CinematicCard theme="paper" title="Equivalence Relation">
  <p>If a relation is Reflexive, Symmetric, AND Transitive, it is called an <strong>Equivalence Relation</strong>.</p>
  <div class="scribble-circle text-red-500 font-bold mt-4">Important!</div>
</CinematicCard>
